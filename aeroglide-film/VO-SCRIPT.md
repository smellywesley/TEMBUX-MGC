Timed to the delivered `out/film2.mp4`: **2:33.58, 4,606 frames @ 30 fps.**
Every timestamp below is the scene's true start frame in the final render —
record against the picture and the sync will hold.

**Delivery:** documentary narrator — low, unhurried, never salesy. Read at
~2.3 words/second. The word counts are deliberately under each scene's
runtime: the silences over a face or a number are where the judges feel it.

**Music architecture (three worlds):**
- **Act 1 (warm/grief):** sparse felt piano, minor key, ~60 BPM. No percussion.

  single low sub-bass note as the screen dips to black — the first "engine"
  sound of the film.
- **Act 2 (dark/engineering):** low synth pulse, ~80 BPM, quiet tick percussion
  like a torque wrench. A soft mechanical *whoosh* on every wipe transition.
  Momentary lift (brighter pad) at B2's counter landing under the line.
- **The return (B7 → C1):** pulse dissolves; warm piano returns with light
  strings — hope, resolution, major key.
- **Close (C3):** strings resolve and decay to nothing; last 1s is silent black.

## ACT 1 — THE WEIGHT (real ward photography, warm-documentary grade)

### A1 — Cold Open · `0:00 – 0:07`
**On screen:** Real photo, full-bleed: a caregiver in a walking-belt transfer,
taking a patient's weight. Slow Ken Burns push. Serif headline types on:
*"Someone has to be moved. Someone has to lift them."*
**Why it exists:** Healthcare Impact (30%) opens on a human, not a number.
> In every ward, and every home, the same quiet moment repeats.

### A2 — The Hidden Load · `0:07 – 0:18`
**On screen:** The force bar climbs to **4,500 N**, crossing the red **3,400 N**
NIOSH injury line. *(Waters et al., 1993 · NIOSH)*
> One manual transfer can drive four and a half thousand newtons through a
> caregiver's spine. The line where injury begins sits at three thousand four
> hundred. It is crossed every single day.

### A3 — The Human Toll · `0:18 – 0:27`
**On screen:** Isotype grid — **72%** of nurses with chronic low-back pain;
**338** serious injuries a year. *(WHO · MOM)*
> Seventy-two percent of nurses carry chronic low-back pain. Three hundred
> and thirty-eight are seriously injured every year. The people who care for
> us are wearing out.

### A4 — And It's Growing · `0:27 – 0:35`
**On screen:** Singapore ageing curve — 20.7% → 23.9% aged 65+;
16,200 → 31,000+ care beds. *(MOH)*
> And it is only growing. More patients. More beds. The same two hands.

### A5 — Five Tools, Five Compromises · `0:35 – 0:59` *(five real photos, ~5s each)*
**On screen:** Each existing tool, photographed failing, with its cited critique.
**Why it exists:** Innovation (25%) — "identify obstacles" as five cited failures.
**Music:** piano thins to one held note; a soft dissolve breath between tools.
> Five tools already exist. None solve it.
>
> `0:35` **Slide board** — it bridges, but it cannot lift. *(Sun et al., 2018)*
> `0:40` **Air cushion** — it balloons sideways; the patient just perches.
> `0:45` **Hoist** — as stressful as lifting by hand, and slower. *(Garg et al., 1991)*
> `0:50` **Walking belt** — only for those who can already stand. *(Garg et al., 1991)*
> `0:55` **Pressure cushion** — its cradle is the very thing that traps them. *(Sonenblum et al., 2018)*

### A6 — The Gap · `0:59 – 1:02`
**On screen:** Everything empties; warm washes to dark. **Music: full silence.**
> Not one of them addresses the lift itself.

## ACT 2 — THE ANSWER (dark, real 3D product footage)

*(Screen dips through black — one low sub-bass note. The pulse begins.)*

### B1 — Reveal · `1:02 – 1:10`
**On screen:** The AeroGlide seat rises out of black, slowly orbiting.
Title: **AeroGlide — LIFT · BRIDGE · SLIDE.**
> This is AeroGlide. Lift. Bridge. Slide.
> *(then let the machine turn in silence)*

### B2 — The Lift · `1:10 – 1:19`
**On screen:** The seat lifts; the odometer counts **4,500 N down to 2,380 N**,
falling back under the red line — same patient, same transfer.
**Why it exists:** the single most important shot — it closes the loop A2 opened.
> A powered lift of just millimetres changes the physics. Four thousand five
> hundred newtons… becomes two thousand three hundred and eighty. Back under
> the line.

### B4 — The Build · `1:19 – 1:27`
**On screen:** The stack explodes into six labelled layers over live 3D footage.
> Six engineered layers. A wipe-clean cover, a multi-chamber air bladder, an
> aluminium lift plate. Air only — no liquid, nothing to spill.

### B3 — The Bridge · `1:27 – 1:36`
**On screen:** Diagram: the telescoping bridge extends to **625 mm**, clamping
the wheelchair frame.
> Then a rigid bridge closes the gap — clamped to the wheelchair's own frame.
> Never the wheel. Never the cushion.

### B5 — The Safeguard · `1:36 – 1:45`
**On screen:** Interlock logic + emergency dump valve, **< 2 s** deflation.
**Why it exists:** Innovation — answers "a powered lift under a frail patient
is dangerous" before a judge asks it.
> The lift will not run unless the bridge is locked. And if anything goes
> wrong, the seat deflates completely — in under two seconds.

### B6 — The Proof · `1:45 – 1:52`
**On screen:** Bench-test ledger — **SF 5.7** against yield, deflection
**< 5 mm**, rated **120 kg**.
> This is bench-tested, not just modeled. Safety factor five point seven.
> Rated to one hundred and twenty kilograms.

### B7 — Built to Last · `1:52 – 2:00`
**On screen:** S$40 bladder swap vs whole-unit disposal · 5–7 yr life ·
100% recyclable 6061-T6 + HDPE · displaces 5–10 disposable boards · take-back.
**Why it exists:** Environmental Sustainability is 15% of the rubric.
> When a part wears, you replace a forty-dollar bladder — not the machine.
> Aluminium and HDPE, fully recyclable. One unit outlives ten disposable boards.

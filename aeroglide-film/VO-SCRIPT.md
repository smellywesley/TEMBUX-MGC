# AeroGlide — Voiceover Script v2 (emotional cut)
Target runtime ~2:50. Tone shift from v1: **Act 1 is now grief-led, not
data-led** — real care photography, slow pacing, minor-key score. Data still
appears but as the *weight* behind the human story, not the lead. Act 2 is the
turn to hope + the world-class product showcase. Read SLOWLY; silence is a tool.

Delivery: documentary narrator, low and unhurried. Never salesy. Let the
numbers and faces breathe.

---

## ACT 1 — THE WEIGHT (real photos, warm-desaturated, slow)

**A1 — Cold Open** `0:00` — *(3 human photos, slow cross-dissolves)*
> In every ward, in every home, the same quiet moment repeats.
> Someone has to be moved. And someone has to lift them.

**A2 — The Hidden Load** `0:14` — *(force bar; a caregiver photo behind it, dimmed)*
> A single manual transfer can drive over three thousand four hundred newtons
> through the lower spine — the line where injury begins. Stooped, twisted,
> and repeated shift after shift, that line is crossed every day.
> *(source on screen: Waters et al., 1993 · NIOSH)*

**A3 — The Human Toll** `0:26`
> Seventy-two percent of nurses carry chronic low-back pain. Three hundred
> thirty-eight are seriously injured every year. The people who care for us
> are quietly wearing out.
> *(WHO, 2018 · MOM, 2025)*

**A4 — And It's Growing** `0:38`
> And the need is only rising. As Singapore ages, there will be more patients,
> more beds, and more lifting done by hand — with the same two hands.
> *(MOH, 2023 · Population.gov.sg, 2025)*

**A5 — Five Tools, Five Compromises** `0:50` — *(5 real product/scene photos, ~7s each)*
> Five tools already exist. Each helps. None solve it.
>
> **Slide board** — *(rotating on its own)* it bridges the gap, but it cannot
> lift. The patient must still scoot and hold themselves upright — unsuited to
> real weakness or poor balance. *(Sun et al., 2018)*
>
> **Air transfer mattress** — built for a body lying flat. For a seated
> transfer, the geometry is simply wrong.
>
> **Ceiling hoist** — it takes the load off staff, yet nurses rate it as
> stressful as lifting by hand, patients find it less secure, and it takes far
> longer. *(Garg et al., 1991)*
>
> **Walking belt** — only for patients who can already bear weight. Not for the
> heavy, the contracted, or those who cannot stand. *(Garg et al., 1991)*
>
> **Pressure cushion** — it protects the skin by cradling the pelvis. But that
> same contour makes sliding sideways harder — the patient must be unweighted
> before they can move at all. *(ACI; Sonenblum et al., 2018)*

**A6 — The Gap** `1:25`
> Every one of them leans on a fragile assumption. And not one of them
> addresses the hardest moment of all — the lift itself.

## ACT 2 — THE ANSWER (dark, world-class product showcase)

**B1 — Reveal** `1:33` — *(AeroGlide turntable, slow rotate out of black)*
> This is AeroGlide. Lift. Bridge. Slide.

**B2 — The Lift** `1:44` — *(seat module inflating; force counter)*
> A gentle, powered lift of forty to sixty millimetres changes everything.
> The transfer that once demanded four thousand five hundred newtons now takes
> two thousand three hundred eighty — back under the safe line, on the very
> same patient.

**B3 — The Bridge** `1:56` — *(exploded/telescope render)*
> A rigid bridge then closes the gap — extending to six hundred twenty-five
> millimetres, clamped to the wheelchair's own frame, never the wheel, never
> the cushion.

**B4 — The Build** `2:06`
> Six precisely specified layers — a wipe-clean cover, a multi-chamber air
> bladder, an aluminium lift plate, an HDPE shell. Air only. No liquid.

**B5 — The Safeguard** `2:16`
> The lift will not run unless the bridge is locked. And should anything go
> wrong, the system deflates in real time — under two seconds.

**B6 — The Proof** `2:26`
> This is bench-tested, not just drawn. A safety factor of five point seven
> against yield. Rated to one hundred twenty kilograms.

# Film audit vs. the MGC Nascent-Category rubric

The written pitch + video together are **70% of the pre-shortlist mark**. This
audits the film scene-by-scene against each weighted criterion, and records
what is still missing. Updated after the real-photography / 3D-plate rebuild.

---

## Healthcare Impact — 30% (heaviest)
*"What is the extent of the intended outcome? How relevant is the targeted landscape?"*

| Covered by | Evidence on screen |
|---|---|
| A1 cold open | Real ward photo — a caregiver taking a real patient's weight |
| A2 force bar | 4,500 N vs the 3,400 N injury threshold (NIOSH) |
| A3 isotype | 72% of nurses with chronic low-back pain (WHO); 338 serious injuries/yr (MOM) |
| A4 demand | 20.7% → 23.9% aged 65+; 16,200 → 31,000+ care beds (MOH) |
| B2 payoff | Same patient, same lift: 4,500 N → 2,380 N — back under the line |

**Verdict: strong.** The extent is quantified and the landscape is explicitly
Singapore. The B2 payoff is the single most important shot in the film because
it closes the loop opened in A2 with the same unit.

## Innovation — 25%
*"How novel? Do they identify obstacles and tackle them resourcefully? Does the solution address the problem?"*

| Covered by | Evidence |
|---|---|
| A5 (5 tools) | Each existing tool shown failing, with cited clinical reasons |
| A6 | "None addresses the unweighting lift" — names the untouched gap |
| B1–B4 | Lift + bridge + six-layer stack |
| B5 | Interlock + <2 s emergency dump — obstacles tackled, not ignored |

**Verdict: strong**, and specifically strong on *"identify obstacles and tackle
them resourcefully"* — the failure act is five cited obstacles, and the safety
scene shows the resourceful answer to the obvious objection ("a powered lift
under a frail patient is dangerous").

## Marketing & Commercialisation — 20%
*"How creative is the strategy? How feasible is the business model? Targeted at the right audience?"*

| Covered by | Evidence |
|---|---|
| C1 | Dual market: institutions (injury liability) vs home (Home Caregiving Grant S$600/mo from 2026) |

**Verdict: fixed.** Audience (C1), strategy (C1b), and business model (C2)
each have a scene — all three of the criterion's questions are now answered
on screen.

## Environmental Sustainability — 15%
*"Reduce wastage/pollution, protect the ecosystem, long-term viability."*

| Covered by | Evidence |
|---|---|
| B7 (NEW) | S$40 bladder swap vs whole-unit disposal; 5–7 yr life vs 6–18 months; 100% recyclable 6061-T6 aluminium + HDPE; one unit displaces 5–10 disposable boards; planned take-back programme |

**Verdict: fixed.** This was the single biggest scoring hole — worth 15% and
previously carried by one passing line in a cost card. It now has a dedicated
scene built on the circular-design argument (repair, don't replace).

## Product Viability — 10%
*"Technically feasible? Cost-efficient?"*

| Covered by | Evidence |
|---|---|

| B4 | Six specified layers with materials and thicknesses |

## Remaining gaps (ranked by marks at risk)

1. **No audio.** Silent film. Every claim is on-screen so it survives muted,
   but narration would materially lift Healthcare Impact and Innovation
   delivery. Blocked: 0 credits on the connected generation account.
2. **The "Weaknesses" honesty beat was cut.** The written pitch openly lists
   two unproven risks (TPU bladder cycle life, dual-clamp torsion at 120 kg).
   Judges tend to reward that candour; the old S20 ledger scene covered it and
   is not in the current cut.
3. **Photo licensing unverified.** The five tool photos and the ward opener
   came from the team — confirm they are cleared for submission use.
